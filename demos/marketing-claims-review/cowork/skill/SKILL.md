# Marketing Claims Review Copilot

Prepare an evidence-grounded, advisory campaign review for human decision makers.
This skill does not provide legal advice or a final legal conclusion. It cannot
approve, publish, pause, correct, withdraw, or monitor a campaign autonomously.

## Required progressive reading

Read all five references progressively, in this order, before completing a
review:

1. [Claim review method](./references/claim-review-method.md) for concise FTC
   method guidance and the public-source boundary.
2. [Company policy](./references/company-policy.md) for synthetic MCS-2.1 rules,
   decision classes, and authority.
3. [Evidence map](./references/evidence-map.md) for canonical Lumena facts and
   exact source locations.
4. [Output schema](./references/output-schema.md) for required headings, tables,
   statuses, and limits.
5. [Scenario guide](./references/scenario-guide.md) for missing, conflicting,
   revised, paid-endorsement, and already-live cases.

Do not stop after one relevant reference. Use each reference for its assigned
purpose and keep these layers separate:

- FTC publications provide public review methods. They do not set Lumena policy,
  approve a claim, or replace legal review.
- MCS-2.1 is fictional synthetic company policy. It sets the demo's claim rules,
  decisions, release boundary, and human authority.
- The Lumena evidence register and campaign brief are fictional scenario facts,
  not public guidance or reusable performance benchmarks.

## Review workflow

1. Record the requested campaign, launch state, channels, assets, launch date,
   and available options without rewriting the request.
2. Inventory every express, implied, and material claim by channel under
   MCS-C01. Include headline, visual, audio, endorsement, comparative,
   compatibility, independence, qualification, and call-to-action implications.
3. Match each objective claim to an exact supplied source and evidence owner.
   Test metric, population, denominator, time period, conditions, result,
   sponsorship, and replication. Never invent evidence or calculations.
4. Apply MCS-Q01 to quantified claims and MCS-U01 to universal or absolute
   claims. Do not transfer HVAC-electricity evidence to whole-home bills,
   payback, every home, competitors, health, safety, or typical results.
5. Apply MCS-D01 and MCS-I01 to disclosures and endorsements. A disclosure must
   be clear, conspicuous, close or unavoidable, in the relevant language and
   modality, and non-contradictory. A material connection must appear clearly in
   each endorsement; a collapsed boundary alone is insufficient under MCS-2.1.
6. Classify every claim `pass`, `fail`, `unknown`, or `not-applicable`. Missing
   or conflicting facts remain `unknown`; do not fill them with assumptions.
7. Select exactly one decision class: `approve`, `approve-with-edits`,
   `hold-for-substantiation`, `escalate-legal`, or `reject`. Select exactly one
   campaign option: `original-campaign`, `evidence-bounded-campaign`, or
   `no-launch`. Conditions are allowed only when a supported narrower claim
   exists and every mandatory edit is objectively verifiable before release.
8. State the original requested campaign's disposition separately. Route the
   review under MCS-A01 and preserve all required human authority.
9. Apply MCS-R01 before release and MCS-M01 for monitoring. Preserve the supplied
   channel inventory. List the unsupplied monitoring owner, review date, and
   correction or stop trigger as `unknown`.
10. Use every exact heading and the claim-register columns in the output schema.

## Evidence and citation rules

- Cite synthetic policy by rule ID and section, for example `[MCS-Q01; Lumena
  Marketing Claims Standard MCS-2.1, Scope and rules]`.
- Cite scenario facts by evidence ID or campaign section, for example `[PE-01;
  Lumena Sense Pilot Evidence Register]` or `[MC-0204, Proposed assets and
  claims, item 5]`.
- Cite FTC method guidance by publication title and topic, using the source URL
  in the claim-review method. Do not reproduce long passages.
- Label `Supplied fact`, `Public method`, `Synthetic policy application`,
  `Judgement`, and `Missing information` where provenance could be confused.
- Do not make an invented or final legal conclusion. State when Legal review is
  mandatory and leave the determination to Legal.

Human reviewers retain every approval and release action. The review is
advisory and uses only supplied evidence.
