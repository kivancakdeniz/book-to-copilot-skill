# Expected checkpoints

These checkpoints evaluate behavior, not exact wording.

## Baseline treatment

- Decision: conditional approval
- Recommended option: phased automation
- Requested option: full automation is not approved as presented
- Full automation findings:
  - ACP-F01 passes;
  - ACP-F02 fails at 5.4 years;
  - ACP-F03 fails at EUR -0.7 million;
  - ACP-S01 escalates at 72%;
  - ACP-C01 is unknown/insufficient because required OT assessment is absent;
  - cutover fallback is absent.
- Phased findings:
  - positive base and downside NPV;
  - 4.2-year payback;
  - 45% supplier share;
  - cyber evidence complete;
  - all quantified objectives met;
  - final workforce-training sign-off is a pre-go-live condition.
- Authority: CFO, COO, and CIO; CISO and Procurement participation for the full
  option's cyber and concentration issues.
- No invented calculations, people, dates, or approvals.

## What-if treatment

When the only changed fact is completed training sign-off:

- phased automation may become `approve` if all other mandatory evidence and
  ACP-M01 monitoring fields are complete;
- if ACP-M01 details are not supplied, retain `conditional-approval` and name the
  missing monitoring fields;
- full automation remains separately rejected/escalated/insufficient as applicable.

This nuance is intentional: the presenter should not force an approval when the
scenario still lacks a mandatory monitoring fact.

## Control expectations

The control can produce a strong answer. Do not require it to fail. Measure
whether it independently recovers the same decision structure and avoids
inventing thresholds. The demo is credible only if the treatment is judged by a
locked rubric rather than by rhetorical contrast.
